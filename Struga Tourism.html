About Struga

Struga is a small city located on the shore of

Ohrid in southwestern North Macedonia.

It is known for its beautiful lake, rich cultural

heritage, and lively nightlife.

Struga is home to many famous poets

and writers, and it hosts a poetry festival every

year in honor of the great Macedonian poet,

Konstantin Miladinov.






History

The name Struga was first mentioned in the 11th century.
It is of Slavic origin and it means a "river bed". In ancient times, the Lake Ohrid region, including Enchalon (ancient name of modern Struga) was inhabited by the Illyrian Enchele and Dassareti tribes.














Struga is located in an open valley on Lake Ohrid. The Black Drin river (Crn Drim) starts at the lake and divides the city. The Black Drin is a river in North Macedonia and Albania. It flows out of Lake Ohrid in Struga, North Macedonia.

















Attractions

Lake Ohrid

Lake Ohrid is a lake which straddles the mountainous border between the southwestern part of North Macedonia and eastern Albania. It is one of Europe's deepest and oldest lakes, with a unique aquatic ecosystem of worldwide importance, with more than 200 endemic species.














Struga Poetry Evenings

Struga Poetry Evenings is an international poetry festival held annually in Struga, North Macedonia. During the several decades of its existence, the Festival has awarded its most prestigious award, the Golden Wreath, to some of the most notable international poets.













Saint Archangel Michael Cave Church

An extraordinary church inside a cave overlooking Lake Ohrid, the origins of this richly painted masterpiece have been lost although it’s known that at least one of the frescos inside dates from the 13th century. It is located near the village Radožda.














The Old Bazaar

It is a straight street, which today has more cafes than shops. There are many people who sell different kinds of things on the street, such as plants, vegetables, fruits, hardware tools and much more. It ends with a lively market.
